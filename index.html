What's new

Four things that make Sportify yours: who you are and what you see, a few questions that set it up for you, and the two ways of placing a layout on the roof.

Profile

Your name and photo, the Simple or Advanced view, your role and the theme — saved on this computer and shared with the Sportify tab in Revit. It also shows which tools this computer has (Unity, SOLIDWORKS, Chrome): what cannot work here is hidden instead of failing.

The quiz

Four short questions — what you want to do, which analyses matter, what you already have about the site, how well you know Sportify — set what is shown first and where Sportify opens. It only sets defaults, and you can take it again any time.

Manual placement

Drag sports and garden pieces onto the roof yourself, in Combine. The rules — clearance, setback, circulation width, entry points — check every move, and suggested spots show where a piece fits.

Algorithmic placement

Say which courts you want and how many, and the packing engine (a port of the Rhino Rooftop Sports Court Planner) places them by rules: a garden band along the edge, courts against the setback first, a clear path to every lift and ramp, the leftover as garden pockets. Apply it to Combine, then change anything by hand.

The rules every layout follows

Whether a layout is placed by hand or by the algorithm, four rules decide whether it's actually usable — the same ones Combine's auto-arrange and suggested spots already respect (see them live, with the same wording, in Combine's Rules step).

Clearance

Minimum gap kept between any two pieces, so there's room to walk between them.

Boundary setback

Minimum gap kept between any piece and the roof edge — a safety margin around the perimeter.

Circulation width

How wide a gap must be to count as a real walkway — narrower gaps don't connect a piece to an entrance, even if nothing overlaps.

Entry points

Every piece needs a walkable path back to at least one entrance.

These four rules aren't one-size-fits-all. Sportify also looks at how much you've actually programmed onto the roof — as the total footprint grows, it recommends wider circulation and more entrances, the same way a real building code scales egress with occupant load. It's a suggestion, not a forced override — the planner still decides. You'll see it live, with an Apply button, in Combine's Rules step once a few pieces are placed.

Documents & files

These four rules decide what counts as a valid layout — auto-arrange and suggested spots both respect them.

Clearance (m)

Minimum gap kept between any two pieces, so there's room to walk between them.

Boundary setback (m)

Minimum gap kept between any piece and the roof edge — a safety margin around the perimeter.

Circulation width (m)

How wide a gap must be to count as a real walkway — narrower gaps don't connect a piece to an entrance, even if nothing overlaps.

Min. entry points

How many entrances the layout needs. Every piece must have a walkable path back to at least one.

Quiet buffer (m)

Minimum gap kept between a wellness/garden zone and a loud court or activity — separate from clearance, which only prevents overlap.
